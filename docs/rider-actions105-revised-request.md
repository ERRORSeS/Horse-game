# OXER TO OXER — PUNITIVE RIDER ACTIONS & TRAINING NARRATIVES 9.0 (REVISED)

## Spurs • Crop • Mouth • Repeated Actions • Greater Narrative Variety • Detailed Action Sequences • No Arbitrary Session Endings

**CODEX IMPLEMENTATION PROMPT — REVISE THE EXISTING TRAINING ACTION SYSTEM**

# 0. OBJECTIVE

Expand the existing proposed **Punish Horse** mechanic with much more detailed training narration and genuinely varied horse responses.

The current narrative examples are too repetitive and often describe only the horse's emotional state.

Instead, the training report should describe:

**What the rider selected → what the horse visibly did → how the exercise changed → what happened afterward.**

The system must reflect the difference between short-term compliance, actual learning, emotional tension and declining performance.

Do not describe physical techniques for hurting horses, provide injury-causing instructions, or turn escalating punishment into an effective way to improve performance.

# 1. BUTTON & ACTION OPTIONS

Keep the existing action:

**Punish Horse**

Categories:
- Spurs
- Crop
- Mouth

Severity labels:
- Mild
- Forceful
- Excessive

These are abstract simulation labels. They do not define real-world force, contact technique, duration or body targets.

Each selected action creates a chronological training-log event.

## 1.1 Repeated clicking

- Do not impose an arbitrary daily click limit.
- Do not impose a fixed number of actions per training session.
- Keep the action panel accessible throughout the session.
- Every click must receive a recorded response.
- Consecutive clicks must not be combined into a fake single action without preserving the history.
- Repeated clicks cannot provide infinitely stacking responsiveness or skill bonuses.
- If the horse develops a serious welfare restriction, subsequent selections may be recorded as unavailable attempts rather than simulated harmful actions.

**No arbitrary "you have clicked too many times, training session ended" mechanic.**

## 1.2 Do not automatically end sessions

Do not automatically terminate a training session because:
- The horse pinned its ears.
- The horse swished its tail.
- The horse became distracted.
- The horse refused an exercise.
- The horse showed temporary tension.
- A routine defensive reaction occurred.

Instead, keep the session interface open.

Possible follow-up activities:
- Regroup.
- Return to a simpler exercise.
- Change training focus.
- Allow the horse to settle.
- Finish the session voluntarily.

Existing serious medical and safety restrictions must still prevent inappropriate ridden activity.

A session can remain open even when a particular exercise cannot safely continue.

---

# 2. NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

Every action-generated report should use the same narrative structure:

**ACTION**
What category and severity the rider selected.

**REACTION**
What the horse actually did.

**EXERCISE CONSEQUENCE**
How rhythm, balance, focus, movement quality or task completion changed.

**FOLLOW-UP**
What occurred afterward, if anything.

Example:

"The rider selects a forceful Crop correction. The horse immediately raises its head and quickens for several strides, but the transition becomes hurried rather than balanced. Its rhythm deteriorates, and the next attempt is noticeably less accurate."

Avoid repeating this exact sentence.

## 2.1 Personality must modify outcomes, not dictate them

A calm horse can sometimes react dramatically.

A sensitive horse can sometimes appear unaffected.

An experienced horse can become frustrated.

A young horse may continue cooperating.

Use personality to adjust probabilities, but draw narratives from a much broader common reaction pool.

Do not give every Hot-Blooded horse the same three lines.

Do not require a specific trait for bucking, rearing, avoidance, loss of rhythm or temporary responsiveness.

## 2.2 Severity and outcome are not the same thing

More severe does not automatically mean a larger or more dramatic reaction.

A Mild selection could still coincide with a defensive response in an already distressed horse.

A Forceful selection could produce little immediately visible change.

Do not make reactions perfectly predictable.

# PART IV — ADDITIONAL VARIATIONS

## 11. AVOID REPEATED NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

The above 60 lines are examples, not the complete pool.

Build a flexible narrative system that can combine:

**Action description**
- The rider selects...
- The rider repeats...
- During the next transition, the rider chooses...
- After the previous unsuccessful attempt, the rider selects...
- The rider continues with...

**Immediate observable response**
- Quickens briefly.
- Hesitates.
- Pins its ears.
- Swishes its tail.
- Raises its head.
- Loses focus.
- Changes rhythm.
- Continues without noticeable change.
- Responds defensively.
- Becomes less settled in the contact.

**Exercise effect**
- Movement improves briefly but not sustainably.
- Accuracy decreases.
- Rhythm deteriorates.
- Balance decreases.
- No measurable technical change.
- The requested task is interrupted.
- The horse resumes its previous level of performance.

**Follow-up**
- The horse settles afterward.
- Tension persists.
- The next movement becomes less accurate.
- The rider returns to a simpler exercise.
- No additional training progress occurs.
- A welfare concern is recorded where genuinely applicable.

Use natural language variation.

Do not produce mechanical descriptions such as:

"Spurs used. Horse tense. Focus -2."

The training report should read like a coherent account of the session.

## 11.1 Reaction probability

Horse personality influences response probabilities, but no personality should have only one possible response.

Relevant influences:
- Current tension.
- Recent actions.
- Exercise difficulty.
- Fatigue.
- Trust.
- Responsiveness.
- Horse temperament.
- Relevant learned behaviors.
- Random variation.

No action guarantees a particular reaction.

Do not guarantee improved performance based on category or severity.

## 11.2 Narrative memory

Within a single session:
- Avoid repeating identical prose.
- Refer to earlier incidents when relevant.
- Distinguish first actions from repeated actions.
- Reflect meaningful changes in the horse's state.
- Preserve chronological order.

If a player selects the same action repeatedly, the logs should remain varied while accurately representing the resolved events.

## 13.1 Temporary compliance

A response may be quicker for a short period, but:

- Do not award a permanent skill increase from the punitive action.
- Do not guarantee a better exercise score.
- Do not stack speed or responsiveness indefinitely.
- Keep effects linked to actual observed performance.
- Ensure repeated escalating punishment is not the optimal training strategy.

## 13.2 Welfare

If discomfort or a medical concern is genuinely recorded:
- Use the existing horse health system.
- Preserve medical restrictions.
- Record relevant assessment or treatment.
- Do not invent graphic injury descriptions.
- Do not automatically heal the horse.
